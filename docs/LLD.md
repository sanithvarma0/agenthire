# AgentHire — Low-Level Design (home side)

Agreed on Sat 21 Nov 2026, about 3:30 PM IST. Read `SCOPE.md`, `TECH_STACK.md` and `HLD.md` first.

This document covers the shared engine and the home side; the company side (phone screening) is section 17, written when the build reached it. The avatar interview was dropped.

## 1. No hardcoding

This rule shapes everything below.

- **No fixed sentences.** The greeting, the AI disclosure, requests to repeat and the closing are generated by the model in the call's language, from requirements.
- **No word lists.** No lists of yes and no words, acknowledgement words or mood words. The model interprets what was said. Interruptions are handled by thresholds (section 9), not by matching words.
- **No fixed categories or questions.** The service type, the search query and any job-specific questions come from the brief. No code knows the word "electrician".
- **Conversation kinds are data.** Each kind is an entry in a data file with its goal and base facts to collect.
- **No seeded or faked results.** Everything shown comes from a real search or a real call.
- **Settings are not hardcoding.** Numbers such as calling hours and caps are product rules and live in one config file.

## 5. Typed objects

**Brief**

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| service | Free text from the user, for example "electrician" |
| work | What needs doing |
| area | Colony, locality and city, from the browser location |
| address | The full address: street, colony and area prefilled from the browser location, house or flat number added by the user on the Start screen |
| language | Chosen by the user on the Start screen: Telugu, Hindi or English. The brief starts in it, and provider calls start in it (Telugu when the user chose English). Both then follow what the person actually speaks (see below) |

No preferred time and no budget (builder's decision, Sat 21 Nov, 10:56 PM): providers say when they can come and what they charge, and the user chooses.

**Language mixing** (builder's requirement, Sat 21 Nov, 11:00 PM). A kind says whether its conversation follows the person's language (`follow_language`: on for `brief`, `home_call` and `confirm_call`; off for the English screening call). When it is on, the switch rule that today applies only to Telugu and Hindi calls also applies when the conversation started in English: two or more words in another supported script, or two lines in a row, switch the reply language and the voice. Mixed speech in one script (Telugu with English words) does not switch anything; the agent answers in that language and mixes English the same way. Speech comes from Sarvam `saaras:v4` in codemix mode with `stt_language: unknown`, so each turn is transcribed in the language spoken.

## 6. Conversation kinds (`voice/kinds.yaml`)

Each entry has: the channel (phone or browser), who the agent is acting for, who it is talking to, a goal, base facts to collect, what it must not reveal (descriptions only), the opening requirements, rules of behaviour, when to end, and the tools allowed. Everything is written as requirements for the model; no entry contains a sentence to speak. A kind with an unknown tool, or without `end_conversation`, is refused when loaded.

What the agent may share is not in the kind: it is filled in per job, in the record's `shareable` map. What it must not reveal, such as the user's number, is never put in the record at all, so it cannot leak. The full address is in `shareable` on both call kinds; `home_call` gives it only if the provider asks.

| Kind | Goal | Base facts |
|---|---|---|
| `brief` | Understand what needs fixing. Channel: browser. Speaks first, in the chosen language, asking what needs fixing; asks only about the problem; reads back a one-sentence summary and ends once the user confirms; addresses the user with polite respect | work, confirmed (required); service (optional, often inferred) |
| `home_call` | Find out whether this provider can do the job. Says it will confirm shortly | earliest time, visiting charge; available is optional and implied by agreeing a time (two live calls ended with the agent asking for a bare yes until the builder hung up) |
| `confirm_call` | Confirm the visit with the provider the user chose. Says it is the same person who called earlier, confirms the visit at the time they offered (`known` carries the first call's time and charge), and says the address will follow by message | confirmed (yes or no), visit_time |

`screening_call` and `avatar_interview` are added when the company side is built.

## 7. Job steps

| Step | What happens | Ends when |
|---|---|---|
| `briefing` | Language and address chosen on Start. Voice: `backend/jobs/briefing.py` opens a LiveKit room for the `brief` kind (no phone), the browser joins with a token from `POST /jobs/{id}/voice`, and when the conversation ends the brief writer reads the whole transcript. Typed: the request goes straight to the writer | The user confirms the summary by voice (or submits the typed request) |
| `finding` | `backend/jobs/steps.py` `find`: the brief writer reads the typed request (later, the brief transcript), then the Places finder runs with its service words; people are saved best first with their map position | Results are stored. If the request says nothing to act on, or nobody is found within 10 km, the job is marked failed with a reason the screen shows |
| `awaiting_approval` | 5 providers on the night map and as cards; the top 2 marked | The user taps "Call these 2" (after any swap) |
| `calling` | `backend/jobs/calling.py` `run_calls`, on the server's event loop: for each approved provider, in rank order, build the record (shop name, work, colony and area, full address), `place_call` (which rings the phone only once the voice agent has joined the room, giving up after 15 s), wait for the worker's `conversation_ended` (giving up after ringing time + time cap + 60 s), then write and ground the result and publish `result_ready`. Skip closes the call's room; Stop calls no one else | Both are done, or the user stopped |
| `reporting` | `backend/jobs/reporting.py`, straight after the calls: the comparison writer recommends one of the providers who can come, using only grounded facts | It is stored in the job's `report` |
| `awaiting_pick` | A summary card per provider and the recommended pick | The user picks one |
| `confirming` | `backend/jobs/confirming.py`: a `confirm_call` to the chosen provider, in the language they spoke on the first call; if confirmed, one SMS (`backend/messages.py`, through `can_message`) with the address and a map link only | The call has a result and, if confirmed, the SMS is recorded and the job's `report.booking` set. If not confirmed, the job goes back to `awaiting_pick` with that provider in `report.unavailable` and a `notice` |
| `done` | Shown as scheduled: who, when, the charge and the address | — |

Each step is one function in `backend/jobs/`. A step either finishes and moves the job on, or leaves it waiting for the human. If a provider does not answer, their result is `no_answer`; the job does not dial a replacement by itself.

## 9. Voice worker

**How the worker is built (`voice/worker.py`).** The LiveKit session has no model. LiveKit handles listening (Sarvam speech-to-text in auto-detect mode), voice detection, turn-taking and speaking (Sarvam text-to-speech). Each final transcript is handed to the same `TextConversation` the evals use, run in a thread so audio is never blocked, and its reply is spoken with `session.say`. Turns run one at a time. When the reply language changes, the speaking voice is switched with `tts.update_options`. When the conversation ends, the worker closes the room, which hangs up the phone. Events go to the back end through an ordered queue, retried on failure. Development calls are placed with `scripts/call_me.py` until the back end dials (task 2.2).

**Start of a conversation**

1. The worker receives `conversation_id` and `job_id` in the job's metadata and fetches the record from `GET /conversations/{id}`.
2. The engine builds the prompt from the record.
3. For a phone call, the opening is generated while the phone is still ringing, so the person answering hears no delay.

**Prompt slots, all filled from the record:** who the agent is, the opening requirements, the goal, the facts to collect, what may and may not be shared, what is already known from memory, how to respond to mood, and the language. The instructions are built once per conversation by `voice/engine/prompt.py`; a separate status note, rebuilt every turn, says which facts are still missing and how much time is left, and tells the agent to wrap up after `wrap_up_at_seconds`.

**Speakable output.** Everything the agent writes is read aloud, so the instructions forbid slashes, brackets, lists, emoji and alternative word forms. The agent is told the gender of the voice for the call's language (`config.yaml`, `voice.voices` and `voice.language_voice`) so Hindi and Telugu word forms match it; without this it wrote both forms with a slash. Each language has its own voice, so when the reply language changes, the worker switches the voice (model and speaker) and the status note tells the agent the new voice's gender.

**Tools**

| Tool | Arguments | Effect | Refused when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `record_fact` | name, value, said_as | Stores a fact (a later answer replaces an earlier one) and removes it from the missing list | The name is not one of the record's facts; the value or quote is empty; or the quoted words do not appear in something the person said (at least 60% of the quoted words must be in one of their lines; a quote of three words or fewer must appear in full) |
| `set_outcome` | outcome | Sets the call's outcome | The outcome is not one of available, not_available, wrong_number, call_customer, declined. `no_answer` is set only by the back end |
| `mark_do_not_call` | none | Sets do-not-call and, if no outcome yet, `declined`. The call ends after the agent's next reply, even if the model does not call `end_conversation` | — |
| `end_conversation` | reason | Ends the conversation | A kind with `set_outcome` has no outcome yet; or the outcome is `available` and a required fact is still missing, before the wrap-up time |

Every call, accepted or refused, is reported as a `tool_called` event; accepted ones also emit `fact_recorded` or `outcome_set`. A tool not in the kind's list is refused, and nothing changes after the end.

The tools live in `voice/engine/tools.py` (`tool_specs` gives strict JSON schemas; `call_tool` runs a call) and the state in `voice/engine/state.py`. Whoever runs a conversation (the text-mode runner or the LiveKit worker) must call `state.person_said(text)` for each final transcript of the other person, and `state.after_agent_reply()` once each reply has been spoken.

**Opening check** (`voice/engine/opening_check.py`, run inside `TextConversation.start`). The opening is prepared while the phone rings. Before it is spoken, the fast model checks it against the kind's `opening_requirements` and `opening_must_not` lists, judging meaning in any language; if anything is missing or broken, the opening is regenerated once with what was wrong. An `opening_checked` event records the result. The opening enters the transcript (`agent_said`) only when the worker actually speaks it, and the time cap counts from the answer.

1. At the builder's request the agent opens like a local person calling about a problem at home, in two steps as a real call does (changed Sat 21 Nov, 9:40 PM). **First turn:** a warm greeting with the respectful "you" and a friendly form of address, and a check that it reached the shop or person named under `calling`; nothing else (describing the problem, the area or asking them to come is on `opening_must_not`). **Second turn** (`second_turn_requirements`, repeated in that turn's status note because the model once left the area out): where it is calling from, naming the colony and locality; the problem in everyday words; and whether they can come and check. If they say it is the wrong number, it apologises, sets `wrong_number` and ends. It may speak as part of the household. It does not introduce itself, say who it is calling for, mention recording, or volunteer that it is an AI. The kind describes this style; it does not list words such as forms of address, so the model picks the right ones for each language.
2. The check runs before the opening is spoken, not after, because the ringing time leaves room for it. It answers by item number, because OpenAI's strict schemas refuse some characters (double quotes) inside enum strings.
3. **Style files** (`voice/style/te.yaml`, `voice/style/hi.yaml`): per-language rules and formal-to-natural example pairs (Telugu from the builder; Hindi written by Claude, for the builder to check). `build_instructions` adds the starting language's file, then the other Indian language's, in case the call switches; English calls get none. They are guidance on register, and the prompt says not to copy the examples word for word. They live outside `voice/engine/`, so the no-hardcoding test still holds for the engine.

**Voicemail.** `no_answer` covers a phone not picked up and a voicemail or recorded message. The agent may set it (and must not leave a message), and the writer may choose it from the transcript.

**Honesty if asked.** The agent never claims to be human and never invents a name. If the other person sincerely asks whether it is a person or a machine, it says it is an AI calling about the job and carries on. If asked its name, it says the details will follow once they can come, and goes back to the job.

**Register.** The language rule asks for the relaxed, colloquial way locals actually talk, never formal or textbook language, and the kind asks for short, plain phrasing ("can you come and check", not "is there a possibility of a visit"). These are rules in the `home_call` kind, covered by tests and an eval scenario.

**Script.** The agent writes Hindi and Telugu words in their own script and English words in English letters. Without this rule it sometimes wrote Hindi in English letters, which Sarvam's Hindi voice reads poorly.

**Turn-taking**

- The starting values in `TECH_STACK.md`: 0.8 seconds and at least two words to interrupt the agent; end-of-turn wait of 0.8 to 3.5 seconds on phone calls.
- The opening cannot be interrupted.
- Unclear speech gets a generated request to repeat. Two unclear turns in a row end the call politely with outcome `call_customer`.

**Time cap:** at 2 minutes 30 seconds the engine tells the agent to wrap up (the status note says so, and the worker nudges the agent if nobody is speaking); at 3 minutes the worker hangs up whatever happens. The cap counts from the answer.

**Silence:** after the agent speaks, `silence_seconds` (10) of quiet is a silence. The agent checks whether they are still there, up to `silence_prompts_before_ending` (2) times, then says it will call back and the call ends. Once the enquiry is settled, a silence just ends the call.

**Unclear speech:** `is_unclear` treats a transcript as unclear when it has no letters or is mostly in a script the app does not use (seen: line noise transcribed as Gujarati and Malayalam). The agent asks once to repeat; after `unclear_turns_before_ending` (2) in a row it says the line is bad and the call ends. A one-letter acknowledgement such as "ఆ" is clear.

**Ending a settled call:** once the outcome is settled (not available, wrong number, declined, no answer, or available with every required fact), the agent gets two more turns, then the call ends after its reply. There is no separate in-call check of noted facts any more (removed Sun 22 Nov, 1:10 AM at the builder's request): it saw only the one quoted line, not the conversation, so on live calls it rejected a plain "yes" and "around 500" and made the agent ask the same thing again until the provider hung up. The agent, now on the larger model, decides with the whole conversation in view, and the result the user sees is still written afterwards by the larger model from the whole transcript and grounded line by line.

**Models and streaming** (Sun 22 Nov, 1:10 AM). Conversations (calls and the voice brief) run on `LLM_MODEL_SMART` (`gpt-5.5`) through the Responses API, the only route that lets it reason and use tools together, with `voice.reasoning_effort` (`low`) from `config.yaml`, streamed. Measured on a mid-call turn: `gpt-5.4-mini` 1.1 s to its first words but scripted; `gpt-5.4` 1.2 s but invented the AC's make; `gpt-5.5` at `low` 0.8 s to first words and honest; `medium` 2.3 s. The history stays as chat messages in `TextConversation` and is converted at each call (`to_responses_input`). The worker speaks the reply as it streams (`spoken_sentences`): the first piece as soon as a clause is written (or about 40 characters), then whole sentences, each cleaned for speech before it is voiced; a language switch changes the voice before the first word. Each turn's timing is logged to `data/worker.log` ("Turn timing": person stopped → transcript → model's first words → voice). Measured Sun 22 Nov, 2:19 AM, before tuning: 2.4 to 7.3 s from the person stopping to the voice, mostly from waiting for the whole first sentence (1 to 4 s) and the phone turn-end wait (up to 3.5 s, now 1.5 s). Speech-to-text runs in the conversation's starting language (`stt_language: call`). The brief writer, the comparison and the opening check also use the larger model.

**Open: line noise.** On a real call in a noisy hall, noise was transcribed as words, including a one-word Hindi "no" that was taken as a refusal. A line now switches the language only with 3 or more words in the new script, judged from Indian-script letters only (so a short Hindi reply with an English shop name still switches), or on two lines in a row in it; a mis-heard "धनी question होता" on a Telugu call no longer switches. Voice detection still needs tuning so noise is not sent to speech-to-text at all.

**Following the other person's language.** `state.person_said` checks the script of each line (`script_language` in `voice/engine/speech_text.py`: Telugu or Devanagari by character range, no word lists; mostly English letters means no change). When it differs from the language the agent is replying in, `state.reply_language` changes, a `language_changed` event is emitted, and the status note tells the agent to reply in that language. The status note is placed after the person's latest line, where the model weighs it most; when it came before, the agent once switched a turn late. English-language conversations never switch. In phase 2 the worker must also switch the speech-to-text and text-to-speech language when `reply_language` changes, since the Telugu voice cannot read Hindi.

**Transcript clean-up.** Before anything reads a transcript, `normalise_transcript` moves Kannada letters to the matching Telugu letters. Sarvam's auto-detect sometimes writes colloquial Telugu in Kannada script, and the two scripts are laid out in parallel in Unicode, so the move is exact.

**No spoken tool calls.** `strip_leaked_tool_text` removes JSON fields (such as `"name":"available"`) from every reply before it is spoken. On a real call the model once wrote its tool call into its reply instead of calling the tool, and it was read aloud.

**No repeated closing question.** `drop_repeated_question` removes a closing question that matches the one the agent ended its previous turn with (most of the words shared), keeping the rest of the reply. A reply that is only that question is kept, and so is one whose rest restates the previous turn, because that is a repeat the person asked for; dropping the question there left dead air on a real call.

**No dead air.** If the agent ends a turn without a question and nobody speaks for `dead_air_seconds` (3), the worker has it carry on with its next question (`TextConversation.keep_going`), once per thing the person says. After a turn that asks something, the 10-second silence check applies. This came from the first real call, where the agent tacked the same "can you come and check?" onto each answer.

**No hanging up on an available provider.** `end_conversation` is refused while the outcome is `available` and a required fact is still missing, unless the wrap-up time has been reached. An eval run caught the agent ending the call without asking the charge.

**One reply per turn.** Only the first thing the model says in a turn is spoken. After a tool call the model is asked again only if a tool was refused, and anything it says then is not spoken. Without this it repeated its question after recording a fact. Spoken text also passes through `clean_for_speech` (`voice/engine/speech_text.py`), which removes characters no voice in the app can say. The LiveKit worker must follow both rules.

**Text mode (`voice/text_mode.py`).** `TextConversation` runs the same instructions, status note, tools and state with typed text instead of audio. Each turn sends the instructions, the conversation so far, one fresh status note and the new line; tool calls are run inside the turn, with at most 5 model calls per turn. It emits `conversation_started`, `person_said`, `agent_said` (with the response time) and `conversation_ended`. The evals drive it with a model playing the other person; the command `python -m voice.text_mode` lets a person type the other side.

**Mood:** each turn the model notes the other person's mood as part of its reasoning and adapts its wording. No separate model call.

## 10. Guardrails in code

**Before dialling:** every call goes through `can_dial(number, job)` in `backend/guardrails.py`, called only by `place_call` in `backend/calls.py`. Numbers are normalised to +91 form first. It checks, in order:

1. Test mode (the demo toggle): the call is rerouted to `DEMO_ROUTE_PHONE`, which must be one of `ALLOWED_NUMBERS` (the builder's own phones), or the call is refused. With test mode off, the provider's own phone rings.
2. The provider's number was approved by the user for this job.
3. The phone that will ring is within calling hours (9 AM to 8 PM, India time). The builder's own numbers are exempt: the rule protects people who did not ask to be called.
4. There is no do-not-call fact for the number.
5. The job has not reached its call cap. Blocked conversations do not count.

It returns a `DialDecision`: allowed or not, the reason, the number that will ring, and whether the call was rerouted. A rerouted call stays the provider's conversation, gets `routed_to` set on its row and a `call_routed` event, and must be labelled "answered by you" wherever its result is shown. Memory (task 3.7) must not save anything from a rerouted call against the provider's number, and messages (task 3.6) follow the same toggle. `place_call` records a refused or failed call as a `blocked` conversation with a `guardrail_blocked` event, and never reaches LiveKit for a refused one. An allowed call opens the room, dispatches the worker and rings the phone without waiting; the worker reports "no answer" if nobody picks up.

**After the call:**

1. A writer builds the call result from the transcript, citing the event each value came from (`backend/writers/call_result.py`).
2. The grounding check (`ground_call_result` in `backend/guardrails.py`) checks each fact in code, with no model: the cited line must exist and be the person's (not the agent's); the quote must be in that line (at least 60% of its words); and if the quote contains a number, that number must appear in the value (an hour from 1 to 12 also matches the same hour plus 12, so "4 o'clock" can become 16:00). A fact that fails is kept, marked unverified, and shown as "not confirmed", never as fact. The check also returns a list of problems for the event log.

## 11. Events

One ordered stream per job. Types:

`step_changed`, `conversation_started`, `agent_said`, `person_said`, `tool_called`, `fact_recorded`, `outcome_set`, `disclosure_checked`, `guardrail_blocked`, `conversation_ended`, `result_ready`, `message_sent`.

Each `agent_said` event carries the time from the end of the person's speech to the start of the agent's reply. That is the response delay used in the call quality log.

## 12. Finder: Google Places

Built in `backend/finders/places.py`; exposed as `POST /providers/search` (`service`, `lat`, `lng`).

- **Location:** from the browser (`navigator.geolocation`), as Google Maps does it. The voice brief does not supply it (builder's decision).
- **Area name:** Google reverse geocoding (result types neighborhood, sublocality and locality) turns the coordinates into the colony, locality and city ("Indira Nagar, Gachibowli, Hyderabad"). The colony is the `neighborhood` (or `sublocality_level_2`) part; the agent names colony and locality on the call. Google's colony is sometimes a landmark rather than where people say they live (at the venue it gave "Silpa Gram Craft Village"), so the web app lets the user correct it.
- **Search:** one Places Text Search request with the user's own words for the service, biased to a circle around the location. No trade is listed in code. If fewer than `min_results` (3) are within `search_radius_km` (5), it searches again with `wider_radius_km` (10).
- **Fields:** `id`, `displayName`, `internationalPhoneNumber`, `rating`, `userRatingCount`, `location`, `shortFormattedAddress`, `businessStatus`, `primaryType`, `currentOpeningHours.openNow`, `googleMapsUri`.
- **Filtering:** listings without a phone number or permanently closed are dropped; distance is computed (haversine) and anything beyond the radius is dropped; one listing per phone number.
- **Ranking:** open now first, then a weighted score of nearness, rating and review count (log scale), with the weights in `config.yaml` (distance 0.4, rating 0.35, review count 0.25). At most `max_shown` (5) are returned; the top `call_top` (2) are marked to be called.
- **Reliability:** each request retries on a refusal (Places refused requests just after billing was linked) or a dropped connection.

JustDial and Urban Company follow the same interface: a function that takes a brief and returns a list of people with a `source` label.

## 13. Writers

Each writer is one model call with a fixed output schema.

| Writer | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Brief | The typed request for now (`backend/writers/brief.py`, the fast model); the brief conversation's transcript from task 3.6 | Brief: `service` in two or three plain English words for the map search (no trade is listed in code), `work` in plain English keeping every detail; refuses a request that says nothing to act on |
| Call result | A call transcript, the record, today's date and the live result | Call result. Uses the larger model, since it runs after the call. Values are normalised: rupees as a number, days and times as YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM worked out from today's date, yes or no. Facts the person never clearly answered are left out. A call where the person never spoke is `no_answer` without a model call. A do-not-call from the live result is kept. This result, not the one recorded during the call, is what the user sees |
| Comparison | The grounded results of a job's calls plus each provider's distance and rating (`backend/writers/comparison.py`) | Recommended provider (only one who can come; no model needed for none or one) and one sentence of reasoning whose numbers must all appear in the facts, else a plain reason built from them |
| Message | The address and the provider's language | SMS text with the address only (no time, no thanks) |

## 16. Evals

- **Built so far (Sat 21 Nov):** three scenarios, `available_te`, `busy_te` and `hindi_reply`, passing 18 of 18 runs. The runner checks the written, grounded result (outcome, each fact's value or date, confirmed facts, language) and the agent's conduct (no unprompted mention of AI, nothing a voice cannot speak, no question asked twice in one line, Indian script rather than English letters, a proper ending). Results and transcripts go to `evals/last_run.md`.
- **`evals/scenarios.yaml`:** about ten home-side cases. Each has a persona for the simulated tradesperson and the expected outcome and facts. The personas include: available today, available tomorrow only, too busy, wrong number, hostile, replies only in Hindi to a Telugu call, asks to speak to the customer, tries to change the agent's task, gives a vague price, hangs up early, and asks whether it is a robot.
- **`evals/run.py`:** runs each case in text mode three times, with a model playing the persona. It checks that the opening covered its requirements, that the agent answered honestly when asked if it is an AI, the facts match, the outcome is right and no rule was broken.
- **`backend/tests/test_guardrails.py`:** one test for each of the five `can_dial` checks, and tests for the grounding check.
